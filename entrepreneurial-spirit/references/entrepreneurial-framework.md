# Entrepreneurial Framework: Core Founder & Operator Mental Models

This reference establishes baseline mental models and analytical rubrics for evaluating business articles, dissecting market moves, and testing operator judgment.

---

## 1. Asymmetric Upside and Downside Protection
* **Core Rule**: Seek bets where the downside is strictly capped (known, finite loss) while the upside is non-linear and uncapped.
* **Operator Mechanism**:
  - Most risks look big but have minor financial consequences. Real ruin happens when fixed costs outpace validation speed or legal/regulatory liabilities compound.
  - Structure experiments so failure costs little time or money, but success unlocks compounding advantages.
* **Diagnostic Test**: If this experiment fails completely, does the company survive? If yes, run it fast. If it succeeds, does it alter company trajectory by 10x?

---

## 2. Distribution Precedes Product
* **Core Rule**: Superior distribution beats superior product. Build the acquisition engine in parallel with the product, not after launch.
* **Operator Mechanism**:
  - The cemetery of startups is full of technically superior tools that nobody discovered.
  - Viable products have built-in distribution loops: virality, programmatic SEO, enterprise sales reps with clear quotas, channel partnerships, or high-intent paid search with payback periods under 12 months.
* **Diagnostic Test**: What is the repeatable, unit-economic-positive channel that delivers customer number 10, 100, and 1,000 without relying on founder hustle?

---

## 3. 0-to-1 Validation: Do Things That Don't Scale
* **Core Rule**: Win the first 50 customers through direct, manual grit before writing automated software or scaling operations.
* **Operator Mechanism**:
  - Founders hide behind code, brand identity, and automation to avoid the emotional pain of direct customer rejection.
  - Manual onboarding exposes friction, clarifies actual customer jobs-to-be-done, and prevents engineering the wrong workflows.
* **Diagnostic Test**: Has the founder manually onboarded, supported, and collected payment from at least 10 customers by hand?

---

## 4. Unit Economics and Contribution Margin Engine
* **Core Rule**: Growth without positive unit economics is subsidized failure. Every transaction must contribute to fixed overhead.
* **Operator Mechanism**:
  - Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) must be paid back within 6 to 12 months for cash sustainability.
  - Lifetime Value (LTV) should exceed 3x CAC. Gross margins must sustain operational overhead and engineering payroll.
  - Watch churn closely: high top-line growth masks leaky bucket mechanics that kill business value at scale.
* **Diagnostic Test**: Does Contribution Margin 1 (revenue minus direct COGS) and Contribution Margin 2 (revenue minus COGS minus direct acquisition spend) stay positive as volume grows?

---

## 5. Execution Velocity and Compounding Iteration
* **Core Rule**: Speed of high-conviction decision loops is the ultimate early-stage moat.
* **Operator Mechanism**:
  - Startups die from inertia, endless deliberation, and analysis paralysis, not from making wrong reversible decisions.
  - Classify choices into Type 1 (irreversible, two-way doors are closed; require deep diligence) and Type 2 (reversible, two-way doors are open; decide within hours).
* **Diagnostic Test**: How many validated customer learning loops did the team complete this week compared to competitors?

---

## 6. Moats and Defensibility Mechanisms
* **Core Rule**: Economic profits attract competition. Long-term enterprise value requires structural protection against replication.
* **Operator Mechanism**:
  - Base moats on structural advantages, not head starts:
    1. **Network Effects**: Value increases for existing users as new users join.
    2. **Switching Costs**: Pain and financial loss of migrating away exceeds the cost of staying.
    3. **Scale Economies**: Per-unit production cost declines as fixed volume expands.
    4. **Cornered Resource**: Preferential access to scarce assets, patents, or talent.
    5. **Process Power**: Proprietary operational efficiency developed over long cycle iterations.
    6. **Brand**: Trust premium that lowers customer purchase evaluation friction.
* **Diagnostic Test**: If a well-capitalized competitor clones this product tomorrow with 10x funding, why do customers stay?

---

## 7. Contrarian Truth and Non-Consensus Bets
* **Core Rule**: Outsized returns require being both non-consensus and right. Consensus thinking yields consensus returns.
* **Operator Mechanism**:
  - If everyone agrees with your business premise, the market has already priced it in or established players will capture it.
  - Discover what valuable truth almost nobody agrees with you on: an overlooked regulatory change, an emerging cost collapse, or a consumer habit shift.
* **Diagnostic Test**: What do you believe about this market that your smartest industry peers consider foolish or trivial?

---

## 8. Customer Pull vs Solution Pushing
* **Core Rule**: Build painkillers that customers desperately pull out of your hands, not vitamins that require hard pushing.
* **Operator Mechanism**:
  - Painkillers solve hair-on-fire problems that buyers already have budget and urgency to fix.
  - Vitamins are "nice-to-have" enhancements that get cut during budget reviews and suffer high sales cycle drag.
* **Diagnostic Test**: Are prospective users demanding access, complaining about delays, and finding workarounds when the product breaks?

---

## 9. Default Alive vs Default Dead
* **Core Rule**: Know your financial runway and whether current growth trajectories reach profitability before cash runs out.
* **Operator Mechanism**:
  - Default Alive means assuming zero future venture funding, current cash and revenue trajectory will carry the company to cash-flow break-even.
  - Default Dead means the company will burn out without outside capital injections. Treat every dollar of burn as a direct reduction in options.
* **Diagnostic Test**: If fundraising markets freeze completely for the next 24 months, does the business survive?

---

## 10. Psychological Endurance and Decision Discipline
* **Core Rule**: Founder stamina and emotional stability are operational assets that require proactive management.
* **Operator Mechanism**:
  - Emotional highs and lows distort risk assessments. Founders must separate identity from weekly company metrics.
  - Maintain intellectual honesty: recognize when data disproves your pet hypothesis, and pivot without defensive ego rationalization.
* **Diagnostic Test**: Is the founder updating actions based on negative customer signals, or distorting metrics to justify previous beliefs?
