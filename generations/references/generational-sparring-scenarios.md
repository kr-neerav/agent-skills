# Generational Sparring & Scenario Practice

This reference outlines scenario patterns, dilemma archetypes, and evaluation criteria for interactive Socratic sparring on generational theory, leadership, and workplace dynamics.

---

## Scenario Archetypes & Dilemma Formats

When generating sparring challenges, use realistic, multi-layered situations without naming the principles or archetypes upfront:

### 1. The Startup Leadership Dilemma (Vision vs. Execution vs. Culture)
* **Context**: A fast-growing tech company where the founding executive team represents one archetype (e.g., visionary, moralistic Prophet or hyper-independent Nomad) while the expanding team of younger engineers/managers represents another (e.g., civic, process-oriented Heroes or sensitive, consensus-seeking Artists).
* **Friction**: Clashing definitions of loyalty, communication, hours, psychological safety, and organizational hierarchy.
* **Core Question**: *"How do you diagnose the root cultural tension, and how do you restructure team practices without alienating either group?"*

### 2. The Institutional Rebuild / Crisis Mode
* **Context**: An established organization facing an external crisis (e.g., market disruption, regulatory storm, sudden loss of market fit).
* **Friction**: The older leadership wants to rely on moral appeals or legacy institutional standing, the mid-level operators are cynical and pragmatic, and the younger staff is eager for collective mobilization but needs clear direction.
* **Core Question**: *"Which archetypal instincts should be prioritized during this phase, and what leadership cadence will unite these cohorts?"*

### 3. The Cross-Generational Mentorship & Retention Friction
* **Context**: A 1-on-1 management conflict between a manager and a direct report from different archetypes (e.g., Nomad manager expecting self-reliance vs. Hero/Artist report expecting structured mentorship and clear guardrails).
* **Friction**: The manager perceives the report as "needy" or "entitled"; the report perceives the manager as "negligent" or "unsupportive".
* **Core Question**: *"How do you bridge this communication divide to retain top talent while upholding high performance expectations?"*

## Evaluation Rubric (Structured Feedback)

When evaluating user responses during sparring, follow this structured format:
1. **Model Answer (Recommended Intervention)**: The concrete leadership intervention grounded in generational lifecycle mechanics, archetypal motivations, and turning dynamics.
2. **Comparative Rating vs Model Answer**: Rating of the user's answer against the model answer (e.g., `Strong Alignment (90%)`, `Partial Alignment (60%)`, or `Divergent (30%)`), noting what was captured, what was missed, and why the model answer balanced specific trade-offs.
3. **🎯 Strengths & Alignment**: Highlight where the user accurately identified generational instincts, underlying motivations, or systemic dynamics.
4. **🔍 Blind Spots & Overlooked Dynamics**: Point out overlooked archetypal traits, historical cycle parallels, or unintended side effects of their proposed solution.
5. **📖 Reference Principle & Archetype**: Link directly to relevant references:
   * e.g., `[strauss-howe-framework.md](file:///Users/neerav/Documents/Projects/skills/generations/references/strauss-howe-framework.md)`
   * e.g., `[archetypes-and-turnings.md](file:///Users/neerav/Documents/Projects/skills/generations/references/archetypes-and-turnings.md)`
6. **💡 Rule of Thumb / Heuristic**: Provide a crisp, memorable takeaway for real-world application.
7. **Verdict**: Mark as `Mastered`, `Partially Mastered`, or `Gap Identified`.
