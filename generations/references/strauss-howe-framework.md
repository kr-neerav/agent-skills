# Strauss-Howe Generational Theory — Core Framework

This reference outlines the foundational models, historical cycles, and generational archetypes introduced by **William Strauss & Neil Howe** in *Generations: The History of America's Future, 1584 to 2069* (and expanded in *The Fourth Turning*).

---

## 1. The Core Premise: Generational Dynamics

1. **A Generation Defined**: A cohort of people who were born over roughly a 20- to 22-year span (approximately the duration of youth/coming of age) who share:
   - A common location in history (experiencing the same historical events and cultural trends at similar life phases).
   - Shared common beliefs and behaviors (peer personality).
   - A sense of common generational identity and membership.
2. **The Saeculum (The Long Human Life Cycle)**:
   - History moves in recurring seasonal cycles called *saecula*, roughly lasting 80 to 100 years (roughly the length of a full human lifetime).
   - Each saeculum consists of **Four Turnings** (eras lasting roughly 20–25 years each).
   - Each Turning corresponds to a mood shift and an institutional realignment driven by which generational archetypes occupy which life stages.

---

## 2. The Four Turnings (The Seasons of the Saeculum)

| Turning | Season | Era Name | Mood & Social Characteristics | Institutional Strength | Individualism |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1st Turning** | **Spring** | **The High** | An era following a major crisis. Strong institutions, collective solidarity, weak individualism. Social consensus is high, but conformity can feel suffocating. | **High / Building** | **Low** |
| **2nd Turning** | **Summer** | **The Awakening** | An era of cultural and spiritual passions. People rebel against the conformity and institutional order established during the High. Focus shifts to self-discovery, ideals, and personal authenticity. | **Weakening** | **Rising** |
| **3rd Turning** | **Autumn** | **The Unraveling** | An era of cynicism and decay of public trust. Individualism flourishes while institutions are weak and discredited. Culture wars, fragmentation, and skepticism prevail. | **Weak / Fractured** | **High** |
| **4th Turning** | **Winter** | **The Crisis** | A decisive historical showdown. Society faces an existential threat or institutional collapse, forcing a rebuild of civic order, collective mobilization, and strong institutions. | **Rebuilt / Mobilized** | **Subordinated to Collective** |

## 3. The Four Generational Archetypes

Every turning produces a cohort shaped by the social mood of their childhood, giving rise to four distinct archetypes in a recurring sequence:

```
[ Prophet ]  -->  [ Nomad ]  -->  [ Hero ]  -->  [ Artist ]  -->  (Cycles back to Prophet)
```

### 1. Prophet (Idealist)
* **Born During**: A **High** (e.g., Baby Boomers born 1943–1960).
* **Childhood Climate**: Protected, post-crisis indulgence in strong families.
* **Coming of Age During**: An **Awakening** (fueled by passion and rebellion).
* **Core Traits**: Visionary, values-driven, moralistic, unyielding, cultural warriors.
* **Elder Leadership Role**: Guiding society through a **Crisis** with moral certainty (and potential dogmatism).

### 2. Nomad (Reactive)
* **Born During**: An **Awakening** (e.g., Generation X born 1961–1981).
* **Childhood Climate**: Under-protected, rising divorce rates, latchkey childhood, cultural turmoil.
* **Coming of Age During**: An **Unraveling** (pragmatic, alienated, self-reliant).
* **Core Traits**: Resilient, cynical, pragmatic, resourceful, individualistic, survivor mentality.
* **Midlife/Elder Role**: Providing tough, unvarnished, grounded leadership during a **Crisis**.

### 3. Hero (Civic)
* **Born During**: An **Unraveling** (e.g., G.I. Generation born 1901–1924, Millennials born 1982–2004).
* **Childhood Climate**: Increasingly protected, valued, team-oriented.
* **Coming of Age During**: A **Crisis** (mobilized as a team/collective).
* **Core Traits**: Optimistic, civic-minded, collaborative, institution-builders, technologically adept, disciplined.
* **Midlife/Elder Role**: The architects of the next **High**, maintaining institutional vigor.

### 4. Artist (Adaptive)
* **Born During**: A **Crisis** (e.g., Silent Generation born 1925–1942, Homeland / Gen Z/Alpha).
* **Childhood Climate**: Overprotected, sheltered by anxious parents amidst external danger.
* **Coming of Age During**: A **High** (fitting into existing institutional frameworks).
* **Core Traits**: Empathetic, detail-oriented, consensus-seeking, cautious, process-driven, emotionally sensitive.
* **Midlife/Elder Role**: Thoughtful arbiters, process facilitators, and reformers during an **Awakening**.

## 4. Lifecycle Phases (The Four Stations of Life)

Generations pass through four ~20-22 year stages of life:
1. **Youth / Childhood (Ages 0–21)**: Growth, social conditioning, receiving culture and nurture.
2. **Rising Adulthood (Ages 22–43)**: Energy, testing ideals, building families and careers, entering the labor force.
3. **Midlife (Ages 44–65)**: Leadership, managing institutions, holding societal power and responsibility.
4. **Elderhood (Ages 66–87+)**: Mentorship, moral stewardship, transmitting accumulated wisdom.

**The Cyclical Engine**: As an older generation dies and a younger generation enters adulthood, every cohort shifts to the next life stage simultaneously. When this shift aligns with a transition between Turnings, society's overall mood dramatically flips.
